        How this is calculated

        Seasonal trend: each bar shows how far that month's price usually runs above or below the average of the 12 months around it, averaged over the years shown, for every listed auction in the state together. Measuring against the months around it keeps a rising market from making the late months look better than they were. Thin lines on each bar show the lowest and highest year. A pattern is shown only where every month had sales in at least 3 of the 5 years.

        Holidays: each sale's price as a share of its own auction's average over the 12 months around it, averaged over the sales in each window for each year's holiday, then over the years. The figure includes the usual season (Easter always falls in spring), so it shows what prices did around the holiday, not what the holiday alone added. Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha come about 11 days earlier each year, so their years fall in different seasons. The range is the lowest and highest year. A window needs sales in at least 3 years; USDA's reports go back to 2019, and the most recent months aren't counted until a year of sales surrounds them.

          About these numbers

          Prices come from USDA Agricultural Marketing Service livestock auction reports.

          How these numbers are calculated
          • Goats are Selection 1 or Selection 2, as picked. Slaughter lambs are Choice and Prime plus Choice: some auctions grade most lambs Choice and Prime, others mostly Choice, so both count. Feeder lambs, ewes and rams are every grade reported for that line. Lots the reporter describes (fancy, yearlings, pygmies and others) are left out.
          • Each state is shown the way most of its head sell: per hundredweight (cwt) or by the head. Prices are averaged by head.
          • "All auctions" combines every auction in the state that has reported a sale in the last four months. Each change compares each auction with its own previous sale and with its own sale nearest a year earlier, counting only the auctions that sold that line both times, so an auction missing from one side can't move it.
          • Likely reporting errors and reports USDA published twice for one sale are left out.
